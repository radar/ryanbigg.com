Hands on the wheel

I've been writing software for two decades, and for the last year a good part of that has been done with an AI sitting beside me. People keep asking what I think about it. This is that answer, and I wrote every word of it myself.

The short version: it's a very fast, very confident junior who has read everything and understood some of it. That's useful, provided somebody competent is reviewing the work.

Where it helps

Boring, well-understood work. Migrations, test scaffolding, the fourth form that looks exactly like the previous three.

  • Writing the first draft of tests I'd have written anyway
  • Explaining an unfamiliar codebase back to me
  • Mechanical refactors across 40+ files
The model doesn't get tired, but it also doesn't get nervous, and nervous is a useful feeling when you're touching payments.

Where it doesn't

Anything where the hard part is deciding what to build. Here's the sort of thing it will happily produce:

def charge(amount, card)
  Gateway.charge(amount: amount, card: card)
rescue StandardError
  nil # everything is fine
end

! Rule of thumb: if you couldn't explain the diff to a teammate, you're not ready to merge it.

Guardrails

Keep the tests, the linting, and the code review exactly as they were. The AI doesn't get a lighter process; if anything it gets a stricter one.
